Raptor combines real geographic data with original terrain materials, city geometry, and atmosphere. The source records below identify where the landscape comes from and how it was prepared for flight.

New York terrain and aerial imagery

Elevation: USGS National Map 3D Elevation Program. Aerial imagery: USGS, USDA, The National Map: Orthoimagery, distributed through the USGS NAIP imagery service.

These federal sources are public domain. They provide the 65.536 km region's elevation field and aerial drape. The imagery is modern. The sea floor is a rendering convention, not surveyed bathymetry.

New York buildings

Building locations, footprint alignment, roof heights, ground elevations, and construction years are derived from NYC Office of Technology and Innovation Building Footprints, downloaded September 24, 2026.

The game simplifies footprints into oriented building masses and converts heights from US feet to metres. Facades, roof setbacks, thirteen landmark silhouettes, and six bridges are original procedural game art.

Other terrain sources

Nellis aerial imagery records the USGS/USDA data, geographic alignment, and nearby imagery tiles. Central Valdez elevations records the USGS height and normal data. Scanned rock and snow materials credits the CC0 photographs used for close surface detail.

These environments are visual flight settings. Geographic simplification and authored geometry make them unsuitable for real-world navigation.
